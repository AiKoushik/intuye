# Product vision

## One line

**Feel ready for the Spanish you'll actually need this week.**

Intuye is not a course. It is a coach that pays attention to your life — what you say, what you
talk about, what's on your calendar — and turns it into a small, steady stream of Spanish you can use
tomorrow.

## The problem

Mainstream apps (Duolingo, Babbel, Busuu) teach a curriculum someone else wrote. Learners finish units
about ordering coffee in Madrid and still freeze when their own life asks for something specific: a
dentist appointment, a conversation about their work, a neighbour asking about their kid.

Two things are missing:

1. **Personal relevance.** Words you already *want* to say are processed more deeply and remembered
   better than assigned words. Laufer and Hulstijn's involvement-load account ties retention to
   need, search and evaluation, all of which are highest when the learner generates the need
   (Laufer & Hulstijn, *Incidental vocabulary acquisition in a second language: the construct of
   task-induced involvement*, Applied Linguistics, Mar 2001).
2. **Confidence.** Language anxiety is its own measurable construct and it suppresses performance
   independently of skill (Horwitz, Horwitz & Cope, *Foreign Language Classroom Anxiety*, The Modern
   Language Journal, Summer 1986). Willingness to communicate, not raw proficiency, predicts whether a
   learner actually speaks (MacIntyre, Clément, Dörnyei & Noels, *Conceptualizing Willingness to
   Communicate in a L2*, The Modern Language Journal, Winter 1998).

## Who it's for

- **Primary:** working adults learning Spanish alongside a busy life (A1–B1), who already have an
  iPhone in their pocket all day and would rather learn from their own day than from a fixed path.
- **First user:** the founder — English-fluent, learning Spanish, comfortable mixing languages
  ("me gusta escuchar esto en mi *mind*").

## How it works: the loop

| Stage | What happens | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Hear** | You speak English or Spanglish; you get Spanish back that stays close to *your* sentence, with the new words highlighted | Noticing the gap between what you want to say and what you can say drives acquisition (Schmidt, *The role of consciousness in second language learning*, Applied Linguistics, Jun 1990) |
| **Anticipate** | It spots recurring topics in your speech and upcoming events in your calendar and builds prep packs | Planning *when-then* responses makes a behaviour far more likely to happen in the moment (Gollwitzer, *Implementation intentions*, American Psychologist, Jul 1999) |
| **Rehearse** | Voice role-play of the exact scenario, with rescue phrases and hints | Producing output, not just receiving input, forces the learner to notice gaps (Swain, *Communicative competence: some roles of comprehensible input and comprehensible output*, 1985); mastery experiences are the strongest source of self-efficacy (Bandura, *Self-efficacy*, Psychological Review, Mar 1977) |
| **Retain** | 5–10 words at a time, each with a reason, reviewed on an FSRS schedule | Spaced retrieval beats massed study (Cepeda et al., *Distributed practice in verbal recall tasks*, Psychological Bulletin, May 2006; Roediger & Karpicke, *Test-enhanced learning*, Psychological Science, Mar 2006); FSRS optimises the spacing per item (Ye, Su & Cao, *A Stochastic Shortest Path Algorithm for Optimizing Spaced Repetition Scheduling*, KDD, Aug 2022) |
| **Grow** | A high-frequency base list blends in so your level rises, not just your niche vocabulary | The most frequent few thousand word families cover the bulk of spoken text (Nation, *How large a vocabulary is needed for reading and listening?*, Canadian Modern Language Review, Sep 2006) |

## Product principles

1. **Your words first.** Every screen starts from something you said or something coming up for you.
   The base list fills the gaps; it never leads.
2. **Close to your sentence, both ways.** English or Spanglish → Spanish, and Spanish → English,
   stay as close to your structure and word choice as correct grammar allows, so you can see which
   word maps to which. A more natural phrasing appears only when the close one would sound wrong to a
   native, as one collapsed line.
3. **Brief by default; explain only when asked.** "¿Cómo te va?" gets "How's it going?" — one line,
   no lecture. Grammar, usage or context explanations appear only when you explicitly ask, and cover
   only what you asked.
4. **Preparation, not testing.** No red Xs, no lost hearts, no streak guilt. Mistakes are framed as
   "here's how a native would say it".
5. **Earn every interruption.** Notifications carry a reason ("Dentist tomorrow — 6 phrases ready").
   A hard daily budget and quiet hours are on by default.
6. **Private by architecture.** Audio is transcribed on the phone and never leaves it. Only text goes
   to the server, and you can delete any moment, any day, or everything.
7. **Calm, precise, native.** It should feel like an Apple app: SwiftUI, system materials, Dynamic Type,
   haptics, perceptually even colour.

## Non-goals

- A fixed curriculum, units or levels to "complete".
- Leaderboards, leagues, guilt-based streaks.
- Ads or selling data. Ever.
- Translating other people's speech for them (that's Google Translate's job).
- Android in v1.

## Success metrics

| Metric | Definition | v1 target |
|---|---|---|
| **Spanglish ratio** | Share of English tokens in sentences you attempt in Spanish | Falls week over week |
| **Scenario readiness** | Self-rated can-do (1–5) before vs after rehearsing a scenario | +1.5 points average |
| **Words retained** | Items with FSRS retrievability ≥ 0.9 at 30 days | 70% of introduced items |
| **Weekly active rehearsal** | Users who rehearse at least one scenario per week | 40% of WAU |
| **Notification trust** | Opened ÷ sent for batch notifications | ≥ 35% |
| **D30 retention** | Users active on day 30 | ≥ 25% |

## Name

**Intuye**: Spanish for "senses it", and "sense it!" as an encouragement. The coach senses what
you'll need for what's coming up; you learn to sense what to say instead of translating word by word.

| | |
|---|---|
| Slogan | **Sense it. Say it.** |
| App Store subtitle (≤ 30 characters) | **Spanish in your own words** (25) |
| Pronunciation | in-TOO-yeh; the welcome screen teaches it |

The subtitle says what the app does for search; the slogan carries the name's meaning. Other names
considered: *Entrelíneas* ("between the lines"), *Ensayo* ("rehearsal"), *Listo* ("ready"), *Parla*.
